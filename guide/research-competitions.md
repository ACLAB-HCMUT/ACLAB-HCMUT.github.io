# 8. Research, Lab & Competitions

How to join research, get into a lab, and compete — ways to gain experience beyond the
classroom.

## How to join a lab

- Find a lab that matches your direction (e.g. **ACLAB** for robotics / embedded / edge AI /
  IoT).
- Read up on the lab's research first; prepare the basics (Git, your main language, tools).
- Reach out through official channels; send a short email with your GitHub/CV
  (see [Career Preparation](/guide/career#how-to-write-the-email)).
- Once in: follow [Member Onboarding](/docs/onboarding) and [Lab Rules & Safety](/docs/lab-rules).

## Research vs project-based learning

- **Project-based learning:** build a product with clear deliverables — fast results, great for
  portfolio and jobs.
- **Research:** ask open questions, read papers, experiment, publish — best if you aim for a
  master's/PhD.
- The two reinforce each other; you can start with a project and move toward research.

## Research skills

- **Reading papers:** read the abstract → figures/tables → conclusion first, then the details;
  note "problem – method – result – limitations".
- **Writing reports:** follow Goal → Method → Result → Discussion; see
  [How to document a project](/docs/project-docs).
- **Poster & demo:** one main message, clear figures, a working demo; practice presenting in
  3–5 minutes.

## Competitions

- **Robotics** — on- and off-campus robot competitions.
- **AI / Data** — AI hackathons, Kaggle-style competitions.
- **Innovation** — e.g. **Bách Khoa Innovation: INNOTECH**.
- **CTF / Security** — for the security direction.

## How to find and build a team

- Find teammates with complementary skills (e.g. one backend, one AI, one frontend/communicator).
- Agree on a Git workflow and assign roles early.
- Each member owns a part and can demo their own part.
